VK_EXT_fragment_density_map for Vulkan Foveated Rendering

The VK_EXT_fragment_density_map extension in Vulkan provides a standardized mechanism for variable-rate shading primarily designed to accelerate foveated rendering in virtual and augmented reality applications. By allowing developers to supply a low-resolution 2D map that defines the fragment invocation rate across different regions of a render target, the GPU can concentrate compute-heavy shading operations where visual acuity is highest—such as the center of the user's field of view—while reducing shading density in the periphery.

The Problem It Solves: Fill-Rate Bottlenecks

High-resolution displays in modern XR headsets require rendering millions of pixels at high refresh rates (often 90Hz to 120Hz) to prevent motion sickness. Shading every pixel at native resolution across the entire field of view is computationally expensive. Human vision is sharpest only in a small central area known as the fovea, while peripheral vision is significantly less sensitive to high-frequency visual detail.

Before this extension, implementing foveated rendering typically required complex workarounds, such as multi-pass rendering to multiple viewports with varying resolutions or applying radial lens distortions. These methods introduced significant geometry overhead and pipeline complexity.

How VK_EXT_fragment_density_map Operates

The VK_EXT_fragment_density_map extension introduces an efficient, single-pass hardware solution:

  1. Density Map Input: The application creates a small, 2D view of a density texture (typically formatted with two channels, representing horizontal and vertical density values).
  2. Render Pass Integration: This density map is bound to the render pass via VkRenderPassFragmentDensityMapCreateInfoEXT.
  3. Rasterization Control: During rasterization, the GPU automatically queries the density map to determine the size of the fragment shading invocations for different screen regions. In areas designated with lower density, single fragment shader invocations cover multiple pixels (such as 2x1, 2x2, or 4x4 pixel areas), while high-density areas maintain full 1x1 resolution.
  4. Subsampling and Upscaling: The hardware natively handles the subsampling during shading and expands the results to the full framebuffer resolution without requiring extra post-processing passes.

Support for Fixed and Dynamic Foveated Rendering

The extension accommodates both primary implementations of foveated rendering:

  • Fixed Foveated Rendering (FFR): The density map remains static or centered on the display, optimizing performance based on the assumption that the user looks primarily through the optical center of the headset lenses.
  • Dynamic Foveated Rendering (DFR): When combined with hardware eye-tracking, the density map can be updated dynamically each frame to shift the high-density shading area to match the exact point of the user's current gaze.

Performance Advantages

By decoupling fragment shading frequency from geometry and render target resolution, VK_EXT_fragment_density_map drastically reduces fragment shader executions and memory bandwidth. It achieves this while preserving sharp geometric edges and depth buffers across the entire image, allowing developers to maintain strict frame-rate targets on mobile and standalone VR platforms.
