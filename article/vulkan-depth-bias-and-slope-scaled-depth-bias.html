Vulkan Depth Bias and Slope-Scaled Depth Bias

Depth bias and slope-scaled depth bias are rasterization features in the Vulkan API designed to prevent visual artifacts in shadow mapping, particularly "shadow acne." This article explains how constant depth bias and slope-scaled depth bias work within the Vulkan graphics pipeline, the mathematical formula the GPU uses to compute the bias, and how to configure these values to eliminate self-shadowing artifacts without causing shadows to detach from geometry.

The Problem: Shadow Acne and Peter-Panning

Shadow mapping compares the depth of a fragment against a pre-rendered depth map generated from the light source's perspective. Due to finite depth buffer precision and the discrete resolution of shadow map texels, surfaces can erroneously fail this depth test against themselves. This produces alternating bands of light and dark across flat surfaces, an artifact known as shadow acne or self-shadowing.

Simply shifting the depth evaluation by a large arbitrary offset solves acne but creates a secondary artifact called "peter-panning," where the shadow detaches from the caster and appears to float. Depth bias and slope-scaled depth bias provide a controlled mechanism to shift fragment depths during rasterization to eliminate acne while keeping shadows anchored.

Constant Depth Bias

Constant depth bias applies a fixed numerical offset to every fragment generated by a polygon. In Vulkan, this is controlled by the depthBiasConstantFactor parameter.

The API scales this factor by the minimum resolvable difference (\(r\)) of the depth buffer format. For example, in an UNORM depth buffer, \(r\) is \(2^{-n}\), where \(n\) is the bit depth of the format. For floating-point formats (such as VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT), \(r\) adapts dynamically based on the exponent of the depth value.

While constant depth bias works well for surfaces facing directly toward the light, it quickly fails on surfaces angled away from the light source. On steep surfaces, a single shadow map texel covers a much wider range of depth values, requiring a substantially larger offset.

Slope-Scaled Depth Bias

Slope-scaled depth bias dynamically adjusts the depth offset according to the orientation of the primitive relative to the light. In Vulkan, this is controlled by the depthBiasSlopeFactor parameter.

During rasterization, the GPU calculates the maximum slope (\(m\)) of the polygon's depth across screen-space coordinates:

\[m = \max\left(\left|\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right|, \left|\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right|\right)\]

Surfaces perpendicular to the light direction have a slope of zero, adding no extra bias. As the surface tilts to a grazing angle relative to the light, \(m\) increases significantly, automatically scaling the depth bias up to match the steeper gradient. This prevents shadow acne on angled geometry without requiring an excessively high constant bias that would ruin perpendicular surfaces.

The Vulkan Depth Bias Formula

When depth biasing is enabled, Vulkan modifies the fragment depth (\(z\)) before clipping and depth testing according to the following formula:

\[\text{bias} = (m \times \text{depthBiasSlopeFactor}) + (r \times \text{depthBiasConstantFactor})\]

If depthBiasClamp is non-zero, the API clamps the final calculated offset:

  • If \(\text{depthBiasClamp} > 0\), the offset cannot exceed depthBiasClamp.
  • If \(\text{depthBiasClamp} < 0\), the offset cannot be less than depthBiasClamp.

The resulting bias is then added to the original fragment depth:

\[z_{\text{final}} = z + \text{bias}\]

Implementing Depth Bias in Vulkan

Depth bias is configured in the rasterization state of the graphics pipeline using VkPipelineRasterizationStateCreateInfo:

  • depthBiasEnable: Set to VK_TRUE to activate biasing.
  • depthBiasConstantFactor: The scalar applied to the format's minimum resolvable difference.
  • depthBiasClamp: The maximum or minimum ceiling for the total bias offset.
  • depthBiasSlopeFactor: The scalar applied to the primitive's depth slope.

If the pipeline includes VK_DYNAMIC_STATE_DEPTH_BIAS in VkPipelineDynamicStateCreateInfo, these parameters can be updated dynamically per draw call without rebuilding the pipeline by calling:

vkCmdSetDepthBias(
    commandBuffer,
    depthBiasConstantFactor,
    depthBiasClamp,
    depthBiasSlopeFactor
);

Using dynamic state allows fine-tuning bias parameters per cascade in Cascaded Shadow Maps (CSM), where distant cascades cover larger world areas and typically require higher bias values than near cascades.
