Vulkan Primitive Clipping in Canonical View Volume

This article explains how the Vulkan graphics pipeline performs primitive clipping when geometry extends beyond the canonical view volume. It details the coordinate space where clipping occurs, the specific view volume boundaries defined by the Vulkan specification, how primitives are mathematically clipped and reconstructed, and how depth clamping alters this default behavior.

The Coordinate Space for Clipping

Clipping takes place in homogeneous clip space, immediately following vertex shading (or geometry/tessellation evaluation if active) and prior to perspective division. Performing clipping before the division by the homogeneous component \(w_c\) avoids mathematical singularities, such as division by zero, and prevents geometry with negative \(w_c\) values from projecting incorrectly into Normalized Device Coordinates (NDC).

Vulkan Canonical View Volume Bounds

In Vulkan, the canonical view volume is defined by six clipping planes in clip space coordinates \((x_c, y_c, z_c, w_c)\):

  • \(-w_c \le x_c \le w_c\) (Left and Right planes)
  • \(-w_c \le y_c \le w_c\) (Bottom and Top planes)
  • \(0 \le z_c \le w_c\) (Near and Far planes)

Unlike APIs such as OpenGL that use a \([-1, 1]\) range for depth, Vulkan’s near-to-far depth spans \([0, 1]\) in NDC. If user clip planes are specified in shaders via gl_ClipDistance[], additional half-space tests are evaluated alongside these six standard frustum planes.

Primitive Evaluation and Re-triangulation

During the primitive clipping phase, the pipeline tests each primitive against the bounding planes:

  1. Trivial Acceptance: If all vertices of a primitive satisfy the boundary inequalities for all planes, the primitive passes through unedited to the perspective divide and viewport transformation.
  2. Trivial Rejection: If all vertices fall outside the same bounding plane, the entire primitive is culled and discarded from further processing, saving rasterization work.
  3. Clipping and Reconstruction: If a primitive spans across one or more clipping boundaries, the hardware splits the primitive:
    • Lines: The intersection point between the line segment and the boundary plane is calculated. The portion of the segment outside the volume is discarded, and the outside vertex is replaced by the intersection point.
    • Triangles: Primitives are clipped against each plane using algorithms equivalent to Sutherland-Hodgman polygon clipping. Cutting a triangle against a plane can produce a polygon with up to four or more vertices. The hardware automatically re-triangulates this resulting planar polygon into a triangle fan, introducing new vertices along the clipping boundary.
    • Points: Points are typically trivially accepted or rejected based on whether their center falls within the volume, though point size considerations apply during rasterization.

Attribute Interpolation

When new vertices are generated at the intersection of a primitive edge and a clipping plane, their associated attributes (such as UV coordinates, normals, and colors) are linearly interpolated. The interpolation factor \(t \in [0, 1]\) is determined based on the homogeneous clip-space distance between the two original vertices relative to the clipping plane equation. This ensures that perspective-correct interpolation continues seamlessly during subsequent fragment shading.

Depth Clamping Alternative

Vulkan allows developers to modify the clipping of the near and far depth planes by enabling depthClampEnable in VkPipelineRasterizationStateCreateInfo. When depth clamping is enabled, primitives that intersect or lie beyond the \(0 \le z_c \le w_c\) planes are not clipped by depth; instead, their depth values are clamped to the near or far plane during rasterization. The \(x\) and \(y\) planes remain subject to standard clipping regardless of this setting.
