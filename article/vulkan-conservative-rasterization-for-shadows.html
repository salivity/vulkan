Vulkan Conservative Rasterization for Shadows

Conservative rasterization overestimation, provided in Vulkan via the VK_EXT_conservative_rasterization extension, alters standard pipeline behavior by shading any pixel partially covered by a primitive. In shadow generation algorithms, standard rasterization frequently introduces aliasing, holes, and light leaking due to undersampling thin geometries. This article explores how overestimation conservative rasterization resolves these critical artifacts, optimizes shadow map resolution management, and enhances both traditional rasterized and hybrid ray-traced shadow pipelines.

Preventing Light Bleeding on Thin Geometry

Standard rasterization only generates fragments if a primitive covers a pixel's center (or explicit sample locations in MSAA). Consequently, sub-pixel or thin geometry—such as chain-link fences, foliage, power lines, and sharp edges—often fails to generate fragments in the shadow map. This leads to severe visual artifacts where light bleeds through solid objects or casts detached, flickering shadows.

Using VK_CONSERVATIVE_RASTERIZATION_MODE_OVERESTIMATE_EXT ensures that any pixel touched by a polygon, no matter how small the overlap, generates depth information. In shadow depth passes, this guarantees continuous, unbroken depth silhouettes, entirely eliminating light leaking caused by missed rasterization samples.

Voxelization for Ambient Occlusion and Soft Shadows

Modern shadow techniques often rely on voxelized scene representations, such as Sparse Voxel Octrees (SVO) or Signed Distance Fields (SDF), to compute contact-hardening soft shadows and ambient occlusion.

Voxelization relies on rendering scene geometry from orthographic projections into 3D textures. Without overestimation, thin triangles can slip between voxel centers, creating holes in the voxel volume that let light penetrate sealed rooms. Conservative rasterization guarantees continuous, watertight surface boundaries during voxelization passes in Vulkan, ensuring light cannot penetrate thin barriers.

Virtual Shadow Maps (VSM) Page Marking

Virtual Shadow Maps allocate memory in fixed-size pages, rendering only the tiles visible from the camera's perspective. A critical step in VSM pipelines involves projecting screen-space pixels into light space to mark which shadow pages must be resident.

When projecting fine geometry or distant objects into shadow space, sub-pixel primitives might miss pixel centers during this allocation pass. Overestimating the rasterized footprint guarantees that all required shadow pages are marked for streaming and allocation. This prevents pop-in and missing shadow segments when an object is partially occluded or spans multiple virtual shadow tiles.

Accurate Bounding Hull Generation for Clustered Shadows

In clustered and tiled light culling, engines rasterize light volumes to determine which screen-space clusters or bins are affected by a particular shadow-casting light. Standard rasterization can miss edge clusters if the light volume’s geometric hull does not cover a cluster center, leading to lights and shadows abruptly disappearing at cluster boundaries. Overestimating conservative rasterization provides a conservative broad-phase bounding check, guaranteeing no active light or shadow is culled prematurely.
