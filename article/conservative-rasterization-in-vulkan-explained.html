Conservative Rasterization in Vulkan Explained

Conservative rasterization alters the standard rendering pipeline by generating fragments for any pixel partially touched by a primitive, rather than requiring the pixel center or specific sub-sample points to be covered. This article explores the internal mechanics of conservative rasterization, its configuration via the Vulkan API, and key graphics techniques—such as GPU voxelization and occlusion culling—where this rendering mode is essential.

How Conservative Rasterization Functions

In traditional rasterization, the GPU evaluates whether a triangle covers the exact center of a pixel (or specific sample locations when Multi-Sample Anti-Aliasing is active). If a primitive is small, thin, or grazes an edge without intersecting a sample location, no fragment is generated. This can produce visual artifacts, missing geometry, or gaps in data-dependent algorithms.

Conservative rasterization solves this by broadening the coverage test:

  • Overestimation (Default Conservative Mode): A fragment is generated if any part of the pixel area intersects the primitive. Even a microscopic intersection at the pixel corner guarantees fragment creation.
  • Underestimation: A fragment is generated only if the primitive completely encloses the entire area of the pixel, ensuring that every generated fragment is strictly inside the primitive boundary.

Hardware implementations achieve overestimation by expanding the primitive's edges outward by up to half a pixel (or an implementation-defined dilation value) during the setup phase, ensuring every intersecting pixel passes the rasterization test.

Implementing Conservative Rasterization in Vulkan

In Vulkan, conservative rasterization is exposed via the VK_EXT_conservative_rasterization extension. To use it, applications chain a VkPipelineRasterizationConservativeStateCreateInfoEXT structure into the pNext chain of VkPipelineRasterizationStateCreateInfo during graphics pipeline creation.

Key parameters in this configuration include:

  • conservativeRasterizationMode: Specifies the operation mode, such as VK_CONSERVATIVE_RASTERIZATION_MODE_OVERESTIMATE_EXT or VK_CONSERVATIVE_RASTERIZATION_MODE_UNDERESTIMATE_EXT.
  • extraPrimitiveOverestimationSize: Defines an extra margin (in pixels) added to the primitive boundary, expanding coverage beyond the minimum conservative boundary if required by the application.

Developers must query hardware capabilities through VkPhysicalDeviceConservativeRasterizationPropertiesEXT to inspect limits like maximum overestimation size and whether underestimation is supported.

Practical Applications in Vulkan

1. GPU Voxelization

Real-time global illumination systems (such as Voxel Cone Tracing) and sparse voxel octrees require continuous, watertight 3D representations of a scene. Standard rasterization causes "pinhole" leaks where thin geometry spans between pixel centers without triggering writes. Overestimated conservative rasterization guarantees that all surfaces are completely closed and voxelized without cracks.

2. Occlusion Culling and Hierarchical-Z Generation

When constructing hierarchical depth buffers (Hi-Z) for software occlusion culling, false negatives and false positives must be tightly controlled:

  • Overestimation ensures occludees are conservatively sized, preventing objects from accidentally peeking through cracks and being falsely drawn.
  • Underestimation ensures occluders are strictly contained, guaranteeing that an object is only hidden if it is completely blocked.

3. GPU-Based Collision Detection

Physics engines and simulation systems running on the GPU utilize conservative rasterization to render geometry into uniform spatial grids. Because thin polygons cannot slip through the rasterizer unnoticed, collision detection passes avoid missed-contact anomalies.

4. Shadow Map Optimization

Conservative rasterization is applied to compute tight bounding frustums and sample regions in cascaded shadow maps, ensuring that receiver and caster geometry are fully encompassed without shadow acne or edge-clipping artifacts.
