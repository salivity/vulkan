Vulkan Tier 2 Variable Rate Shading Hardware

Variable Rate Shading (VRS) allows developers to decouple rendering resolution from shading rate, dynamically adjusting pixel shader workloads across a frame to boost performance without degrading perceptual visual fidelity. In the Vulkan API, Tier 2 VRS features—such as screen-space shading rate images and per-primitive shading rates—are exposed primarily through the VK_KHR_fragment_shading_rate extension. This article breaks down the native hardware GPU microarchitectures across desktop and mobile platforms that support Tier 2 Variable Rate Shading in Vulkan.

What Constitutes Tier 2 VRS in Vulkan

While "Tier 1" and "Tier 2" are terms originating from Direct3D 12, the Vulkan equivalent is determined by feature flags within the VK_KHR_fragment_shading_rate extension (and historically VK_NV_shading_rate_image).

Tier 1 only supports per-draw shading rates. Tier 2 adds:

  • Per-primitive shading rates: Triangles can carry shading rate attributes assigned in vertex, geometry, or mesh shaders.
  • Shading rate images (attachments): A screen-space texture or surface defines shading rates per tile (e.g., 8x8 or 16x16 pixel regions).
  • Combiners: Rules to merge primitive, image, and pipeline shading rates (e.g., taking the minimum, maximum, or overriding).

NVIDIA Architectures

NVIDIA introduced hardware-accelerated Variable Rate Shading with its Turing architecture, and all subsequent desktop and data center architectures natively support Tier 2 capabilities in Vulkan:

  • Turing (RTX 20-Series, GTX 16-Series): The first to implement hardware VRS tiles (via NVIDIA-specific extensions and later standardized Vulkan extensions).
  • Ampere (RTX 30-Series): Full native Tier 2 support, retaining 16x16 tile sizing for shading rate attachments.
  • Ada Lovelace (RTX 40-Series): Continues Tier 2 feature support with enhanced scheduling and ray tracing integration.
  • Blackwell (RTX 50-Series / Data Center): Retains full native support for Tier 2 Vulkan fragment shading rate features.

AMD Architectures

AMD introduced native Tier 2 Variable Rate Shading starting with its RDNA 2 architecture:

  • RDNA 2 (Radeon RX 6000 Series, Consoles): Supports screen-space shading rate images (8x8 tile size), per-draw, and per-primitive rates via Vulkan. Powers the custom silicon inside the PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, and Steam Deck.
  • RDNA 3 (Radeon RX 7000 Series): Retains complete Tier 2 hardware compatibility with improved coarse shading efficiency.
  • RDNA 3.5 (Radeon 800M Series / Strix Point): Continues full Tier 2 Vulkan support for modern integrated systems.

Intel Architectures

Intel implemented native Tier 2 Variable Rate Shading starting with its discrete performance graphics lineup:

  • Xe-HPG (Intel Arc Alchemist / A-Series): Fully supports Tier 2 VRS in Vulkan, including shading rate attachments and combiner operations.
  • Xe2 (Intel Arc Battlemage / Lunar Lake): Retains native Tier 2 fragment shading rate capabilities across both discrete cards and low-power integrated chips.

Note: Intel's earlier integrated Xe-LP (Gen12) architecture supports Tier 1 VRS capabilities in Vulkan, but lacks the hardware pipeline required for native Tier 2 shading rate images and combiners.

Mobile and Embedded Architectures

Tier 2 VRS is critical for mobile platforms seeking to optimize power consumption and thermal limits:

  • Qualcomm Adreno: Supported starting with the Adreno 660 (Snapdragon 888) and continued across the Adreno 700 series (Snapdragon 8 Gen 1, Gen 2, Gen 3, and Snapdragon X Elite). Qualcomm supports screen-space shading rate textures and per-primitive controls via Vulkan.
  • ARM Immortalis: Supported beginning with the Immortalis-G715 and newer (such as Immortalis-G720 and Immortalis-G925). These GPUs feature native hardware micro-tile VRS with full shading rate image attachment support.
