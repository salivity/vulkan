Vulkan Ray Tracing Hit Groups Explained

In the Vulkan graphics and compute API, ray tracing hit groups are fundamental structures within the ray tracing pipeline (VK_KHR_ray_tracing_pipeline) that manage how rays interact with scene geometry. A hit group is an organized collection of shaders executed when a ray intersects a primitive. Understanding hit groups is essential for handling rendering tasks such as surface shading, alpha-tested transparency, and custom procedural geometry efficiently on the GPU.

What Is a Hit Group?

A hit group bundles together the shaders responsible for processing ray-primitive intersections. When defining a ray tracing pipeline via VkRayTracingPipelineCreateInfoKHR, individual shaders are indexed and grouped together using VkRayTracingShaderGroupCreateInfoKHR.

During ray traversal, when the hardware determines that a ray intersects a bounding box or a triangle, it looks up the corresponding hit group in the Shader Binding Table (SBT). The hit group then dictates which intersection, filtering, and shading logic to execute.

Hit groups are classified into two primary types based on primitive geometry:

  • Triangle Hit Groups: Used for standard triangular meshes, where primitive intersection is handled automatically by ray tracing hardware or fixed-function units.
  • Procedural Hit Groups: Used for user-defined, non-triangular shapes (such as spheres, hair strands, or metaballs) bounded by Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs), requiring custom intersection math.

Shader Stages Encompassed by Hit Groups

A Vulkan hit group encompasses up to three distinct shader stages:

1. Intersection Shader (VK_SHADER_STAGE_INTERSECTION_BIT_KHR)

The intersection shader is utilized exclusively in procedural hit groups. When a ray enters an AABB bounding volume, this shader runs to compute whether the ray actually intersects the underlying procedural geometry. If a hit is confirmed, the shader reports the hit distance and hit attributes back to the traversal engine using the reportIntersectionEXT function. For standard triangle geometry, an intersection shader is not used because the intersection calculation is performed by built-in hardware tests.

2. Any-Hit Shader (VK_SHADER_STAGE_ANY_HIT_BIT_KHR)

The any-hit shader is an optional stage executed each time a potential intersection occurs along the ray. It executes before a hit is confirmed as the closest intersection. Its primary role is to filter intersections—such as discarding hits for transparent or cut-out materials (e.g., foliage or chain-link fences based on alpha textures). The shader can call ignoreIntersectionEXT to discard the hit and continue ray traversal, or terminateRayEXT to stop further traversal early (often used for ambient occlusion or shadow ray testing).

3. Closest Hit Shader (VK_SHADER_STAGE_CLOSEST_HIT_BIT_KHR)

The closest hit shader executes once ray traversal has completed and the single closest valid intersection along the ray interval has been determined. This stage typically handles material evaluations, lighting calculations, texture sampling, and the generation of secondary rays (such as reflection, refraction, or indirect illumination rays) via the traceRayEXT function. It receives payload data passed down from the ray generation or previous closest hit stage and writes the final computed color or shading attributes back into that payload.

Execution Flow in the Pipeline

When a ray is cast, these stages interact in a defined sequence:

  1. Detection: The hardware identifies an intersection (via the fixed-function unit for triangles or the Intersection Shader for procedural AABBs).
  2. Evaluation: If a candidate hit is found, the Any-Hit Shader runs to validate or ignore the hit.
  3. Resolution: Traversal continues until all primitives are tested. Once the nearest valid hit point is resolved, the Closest Hit Shader executes to perform final shading.
