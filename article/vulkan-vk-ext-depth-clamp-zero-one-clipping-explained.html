Vulkan VK_EXT_depth_clamp_zero_one Clipping Explained

The VK_EXT_depth_clamp_zero_one extension alters how Vulkan handles depth values when depth clamping is enabled, switching the clamping bounds from the viewport's depth range to a fixed range of 0.0 to 1.0. This article explains the standard clipping and clamping pipeline in Vulkan, details the specific operational changes introduced by this extension, and highlights why this modification is essential for emulation layers and graphics portability.

Standard Depth Clipping and Clamping in Vulkan

In standard Vulkan operation, primitive clipping takes place in clip space before rasterization. For depth, primitives are tested against the near and far clip planes defined by the clip-space condition:

\[0 \le z_c \le w_c\]

Any portion of a primitive falling outside this volume is clipped and discarded.

When a pipeline enables depth clamping by setting depthClampEnable = VK_TRUE in VkPipelineRasterizationStateCreateInfo, Vulkan modifies this behavior by disabling the near and far plane clipping stages. Instead of discarding fragments that extend beyond the view frustum along the z-axis, the graphics pipeline rasterizes them. After viewport transformation, the resulting fragment depth values (\(z_f\)) are clamped to the range defined by the current viewport parameters:

\[[\min(minDepth, maxDepth), \max(minDepth, maxDepth)]\]

How VK_EXT_depth_clamp_zero_one Modifies Behavior

The VK_EXT_depth_clamp_zero_one extension introduces a single feature flag, depthClampZeroOne, exposed via VkPhysicalDeviceDepthClampZeroOneFeaturesEXT.

When this feature is enabled:

  1. Near and Far Clipping Remain Disabled: Primitives extending beyond the near (\(z_c < 0\)) and far (\(z_c > w_c\)) planes are not clipped, preserving the core behavior of depthClampEnable.
  2. Fixed Clamping Range: Instead of clamping the post-viewport fragment depth to \([\min(minDepth, maxDepth), \max(minDepth, maxDepth)]\), the pipeline strictly clamps the depth to the fixed numerical range \([0.0, 1.0]\).

This decouples the depth clamping boundary from the minDepth and maxDepth values specified in VkViewport. Even if a viewport is configured with an inverted range (e.g., minDepth = 1.0 and maxDepth = 0.0) or a restricted sub-range (e.g., 0.2 to 0.8), fragments generated beyond the standard view bounds are consistently clamped to \(0.0\) and \(1.0\).

Purpose and Use Cases

The primary motivation for VK_EXT_depth_clamp_zero_one is compatibility with Direct3D. In Direct3D 10, 11, and 12, disabling depth clipping (DepthClipEnable = FALSE) causes out-of-bounds primitives to be rasterized with depth values clamped to the canonical \([0.0, 1.0]\) range, regardless of specific viewport depth transformations.

Prior to this extension, translation layers such as DXVK and VKD3D-Proton faced discrepancies when translating Direct3D workloads that utilized non-standard viewport depth bounds alongside disabled depth clipping. By implementing VK_EXT_depth_clamp_zero_one, Vulkan drivers allow these translation layers and native engines to replicate Direct3D depth handling without requiring costly shader workarounds or manual depth-reconstruction passes.
